# Practical STE vocabulary

This is a **subset**, not the official dictionary. The full ASD-STE100 dictionary
holds roughly 900 approved general words and cannot be reproduced here. What
follows is the high-frequency core — the words that appear constantly in technical
explanations, plus the substitutions that catch the most violations.

## How the dictionary works

STE entries follow a **one word, one part of speech, one meaning** rule. `test` is
approved as a noun (`the test`) and is not approved as a verb (`to test` the
system). `close` is approved as a verb for two specific meanings only — a door, or
an electrical circuit — and is not approved as a near preposition. So before
swapping a word, confirm the part of speech is approved too.

Uppercase in the official dictionary means approved. Lowercase means not approved,
and the entry lists suggested approved alternatives.

## Never flagged

These are approved Technical Names or Technical Verbs under rules 1.5 and 1.12,
which cover domain vocabulary outside the core dictionary. Leave them alone:

- Programming and infrastructure: `asyncio`, `async`, `await`, `idempotent`,
  `commit`, `schema`, `middleware`, `endpoint`, `runtime`, `payload`,
  `dependency injection`, `CDN`, `ORM`, `JWT`, `REST`, `CLI`, `API`, `SDK`,
  `container`, `virtualization`, `repository`, `branch`, `merge`, `diff`,
  `daemon`, `socket`, `buffer`, `cache`, `token`, `cookie`, `header`,
  `environment variable`, `configuration file`
- Mathematical and structural terms: `matrix`, `vector`, `node`, `tree`,
  `depth-first`, `binary`, `hexadecimal`, `iterator`, `callback`, `closure`,
  `monad`
- Product and company names: never correct these, they are proper nouns

The rule of thumb: if the word identifies a specific thing in the domain, it is
approved. If it is a general English word with an approved synonym, swap it.
